Safeguarding & Child Protection

Keeping every Venix NextGen participant safe, online and in person.

Effective Date
16 July 2026 · Version 1.0
Related Documents

1. Statement of commitment

The Venix NextGen Initiative exists to give young people practical, responsible AI skills. Some of our participants are children in law, and all of them place their trust in us. We are committed to ensuring that every young person who takes part in our programmes, online or in person, is safe from harm, treated with dignity, and protected from abuse, exploitation, neglect and harassment of any kind. Safeguarding is not one person's job at Venix NextGen; it is a condition of involvement for everyone who works with us.

2. Purpose and scope

This Policy sets out how we prevent harm, how we recognise and respond to concerns, and what we expect of every adult connected to the Initiative. It applies to:

  • All staff of the Initiative and of Venix Partners Limited when working on the Initiative;
  • All facilitators, mentors, trainers and guest speakers;
  • All volunteers, interns and contractors;
  • Board members and advisers;
  • Partner organisations and their representatives when engaging with our participants.

It covers all programme settings: live in person workshops, virtual sessions, one on one mentoring, messaging groups, social media interactions, events, and any other context in which our people come into contact with participants. It protects all participants, with heightened protections for those under 18, and it also protects vulnerable adults taking part in our programmes.

3. Legal and policy framework

This Policy is grounded in Nigerian law and international standards, including:

  • The Child Rights Act 2003 and the child rights laws of the states in which we operate, including the Child's Rights Law of Lagos State;
  • The Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999 (as amended);
  • The Violence Against Persons (Prohibition) Act 2015;
  • The Cybercrimes (Prohibition, Prevention, etc.) Act 2015 as amended, including its provisions on online offences against children;
  • The Trafficking in Persons (Prohibition) Enforcement and Administration Act 2015;
  • The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, particularly its provisions on the personal data of children;
  • The United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child and the African Charter on the Rights and Welfare of the Child.

For the purposes of this Policy, a "child" is any person under the age of 18, consistent with the Child Rights Act 2003, and "safeguarding" means everything we do to keep participants safe and to act properly when concerns arise.

4. What we protect against

Harm can take many forms, and our people are trained to recognise all of them, including:

  • Physical abuse: any deliberate act causing physical harm to a participant;
  • Emotional abuse: persistent humiliation, intimidation, belittling, isolation or other treatment that damages a young person's wellbeing or self-worth;
  • Sexual abuse and exploitation: any sexual contact, behaviour, communication or material involving a child, and any exploitation of a position of trust for sexual purposes, whatever the age of the participant;
  • Online harm: grooming, inappropriate private contact, cyberbullying, exposure to harmful content, and the misuse of images, recordings or personal data, including harms created or amplified with AI tools such as fabricated images of a participant;
  • Neglect: failure to take reasonable care of participants in settings we control;
  • Bullying, harassment and discrimination: by adults or by other participants, in any programme setting;
  • Financial exploitation: soliciting money, gifts or favours from participants or their families.

5. Guiding principles

  • The best interests of the child come first in every decision that affects a participant under 18;
  • We take a preventive approach: safe recruitment, safe programme design and safe platforms, rather than relying on response alone;
  • Every concern is taken seriously, recorded and acted on, however minor it may appear;
  • We listen to young people, take what they tell us seriously, and involve them in decisions about their own safety wherever possible;
  • We do not discriminate: every participant is equally entitled to protection regardless of gender, ethnicity, religion, disability or background;
  • We cooperate fully with the appropriate authorities and never attempt to investigate criminal matters ourselves;
  • We protect those who raise concerns in good faith from any form of retaliation.

6. Safe recruitment and vetting

Everyone whose role involves contact with participants must complete our vetting process before that contact begins. This includes:

  • Verification of identity with a valid government issued document;
  • At least two references, one of which must be able to speak to the person's conduct around young people or in a position of trust;
  • A signed self declaration confirming that the person has no convictions, pending charges or previous findings relating to the abuse or exploitation of children or vulnerable people, and criminal record checks where available and lawful;
  • A signed acceptance of this Policy and the adult code of conduct in Section 7;
  • Completion of safeguarding induction training before the first session with participants.

A person who declines any element of vetting will not be given a participant facing role. Vetting records are held securely and reviewed when a person returns after a significant break.

7. Code of conduct for adults

Every adult covered by this Policy must:

  • Treat all participants with respect, fairness and patience, and model the responsible behaviour the programme teaches;
  • Conduct all communication with participants through official programme channels: the official messaging groups, the official mentoring platforms and the programme email address;
  • Keep one on one mentoring within the official platform, within agreed hours, with the session logged; where a participant is under 18, sessions must be scheduled through the programme, the parent or guardian must know they are taking place, and another adult must be able to join or review the session;
  • Never contact a participant under 18 privately on personal social media, personal phone numbers or unofficial channels, and never ask a participant to keep any communication secret;
  • Never be alone with a participant under 18 in a closed physical space; in person interactions must take place in open, visible settings;
  • Never use physical punishment, humiliating language, sexualised language or innuendo, or share inappropriate content;
  • Never give personal gifts or money to individual participants, or accept them, outside transparent programme activities such as prizes announced to the whole cohort;
  • Never photograph or record participants outside authorised programme documentation, and never post participant images or work without confirmed consent;
  • Declare any prior personal relationship with a participant or their family to the Designated Safeguarding Lead;
  • Report every concern, allegation or breach of this code, including their own mistakes, promptly through the channels in Section 11.

Breach of this code is treated seriously and may result in removal from the role, termination of any contract, and referral to the authorities where the conduct may be unlawful.

8. Online safety

Much of our programme is delivered online, so digital settings receive the same care as physical ones:

  • Live virtual sessions are hosted by trained facilitators, use waiting rooms or controlled entry, and always have at least two vetted adults present when participants under 18 attend;
  • Messaging groups used for announcements and submissions are official, moderated by programme staff, and used only for programme purposes; group rules are published in the group and enforced;
  • Session recordings are made only where announced in advance, stored securely, used only for programme purposes and retained in line with our Privacy Policy;
  • Participants are taught online safety, including how to recognise grooming, protect their personal data and use AI tools responsibly, as part of the curriculum;
  • We act promptly on cyberbullying or inappropriate conduct between participants in programme spaces, applying the Participant Code of Conduct.

9. Photography, media and consent

We only feature a participant's name, image, voice or work in our materials with informed, documented consent, given by the participant and, where the participant is under 18, by a parent or guardian. Consent is specific about how the material will be used, is never a condition of participation, and can be withdrawn at any time, after which the material is removed from future use as quickly as practicable. We avoid publishing information that would allow a child to be located, such as a home address or school schedule, and we store consent records securely.

10. Data protection for participants under 18

The personal data of participants under 18 is handled in line with the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and our Privacy Policy, with additional restrictions: minimum necessary collection, restricted access limited to staff who need it for their role, parental rights of access and correction, and no publication or sharing without parental consent except where the law requires disclosure to protect the child.

11. Recognising and reporting concerns

11.1 The Designated Safeguarding Lead

The Initiative appoints a Designated Safeguarding Lead ("DSL") and a deputy, whose names and direct contact details are published to all staff, facilitators, mentors, volunteers, participants and parents at the start of every cohort. The DSL is responsible for receiving reports, keeping records, deciding next steps, liaising with authorities and briefing the board on safeguarding matters.

11.2 How to report

Anyone, including a participant, parent or member of the public, can raise a concern by contacting the DSL directly or by emailing programme@venixnextgen.org with the subject line "Safeguarding". Reports may be made verbally, in writing or through any trusted adult in the programme, who must pass the concern to the DSL the same day. Concerns can be raised about anyone, including senior staff and board members; a concern about the DSL goes to the deputy or the board chair.

11.3 What happens next

  • Any immediate danger is addressed first, including contacting emergency services on 112 where necessary;
  • The DSL records the concern within 24 hours, using the participant's own words where a disclosure was made;
  • The DSL assesses the concern and decides on action, which may include internal measures, suspension of an adult from participant facing duties pending review, and referral to external authorities;
  • Where the concern suggests a criminal offence or a child in need of protection, the DSL refers the matter to the appropriate authority without delay, including the Nigeria Police Force, the relevant state Ministry of Youth and Social Development or child protection unit, and, for trafficking or exploitation, the National Agency for the Prohibition of Trafficking in Persons (NAPTIP);
  • We do not conduct our own investigation into potential criminal conduct beyond what is needed to protect participants and preserve information for the authorities;
  • The person who raised the concern receives confirmation that it has been acted on, within the limits of confidentiality.

12. Responding to a disclosure

If a young person tells you about harm, stay calm and listen without interrupting. Take what they say seriously. Reassure them that telling you was the right thing to do. Do not promise secrecy; explain that you must pass the information to the person whose job it is to help. Do not ask leading questions or press for detail; that is the role of trained professionals. Write down what was said, in the young person's own words, as soon as possible, and pass it to the DSL the same day. Do not confront the person the concern is about, and do not discuss the matter with anyone who does not need to know.

13. Confidentiality and record keeping

Safeguarding information is shared strictly on a need to know basis: with the DSL, with those who must act, and with the authorities where required. Records of concerns, decisions and actions are kept securely, separately from general programme records, with access limited to the DSL and deputy. Records are retained for as long as legal guidance requires, given that safeguarding matters can surface long after the event, and are handled in line with the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023. Malicious reports made in bad faith are treated as a disciplinary matter, but a report made in good faith that turns out to be unfounded will never be held against the person who made it.

14. Training and awareness

All adults covered by this Policy complete safeguarding induction before working with participants and refresher training at least annually. Training covers recognising harm, the code of conduct, online safety, responding to disclosures and reporting. Participants and parents receive a plain language summary of this Policy at enrolment, including who the DSL is and how to raise a concern, and the summary is presented in an age appropriate way for younger participants.

15. Governance, monitoring and review

The board receives a safeguarding report at every scheduled board meeting, covering concerns raised, actions taken and lessons learned, with identities protected. This Policy is reviewed at least annually, and immediately after any serious incident or relevant change in law, by the DSL and approved by the board. Compliance with this Policy is a standing condition of every staff, facilitator, mentor, volunteer and partner engagement, and is written into the relevant agreements.

This Policy should be read together with our Privacy Policy, Terms of Use and Participant Code of Conduct. Emergency: dial 112. Safeguarding contact: programme@venixnextgen.org (subject line "Safeguarding").
