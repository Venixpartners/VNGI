Responsible AI

How Venix NextGen participants are expected to use AI tools honestly and responsibly, and the community standards that go with it.

Effective Date
16 July 2026 · Version 1.0
Related Documents
Privacy Policy Terms of Use Cookie Policy Safeguarding Policy Responsible AI

1. Purpose

This Responsible AI & Participant Code of Conduct ("Code") sets out how we expect participants of the Venix NextGen Initiative to behave in every programme space: live workshops, virtual sessions, mentoring, messaging groups, the alumni community and any event we host. It exists to keep our community safe, honest and worth belonging to. By accepting a place on a cohort you agree to this Code, and where you are under 18 your parent or guardian agrees to it on your behalf.

2. Our community values

Everything in this Code flows from the values of the Initiative: accessibility, responsibility, practicality, empowerment, community and credibility. In practice that means we learn together, we are honest about what we know and what we produced, we treat each other with respect, and we use technology, especially AI, in ways we would be proud to explain.

3. Expected behaviour

  • Treat every participant, facilitator, mentor and staff member with courtesy and respect, in person and online;
  • Attend the sessions of your cohort, arrive on time, and tell the programme team in advance if you cannot attend;
  • Complete assignments and the capstone project honestly and to the best of your ability;
  • Give constructive feedback and receive it graciously; critique work, not people;
  • Respect the privacy of others: do not share personal information, messages, images or recordings of other participants outside the programme without their clear permission;
  • Use programme platforms and materials only for programme purposes;
  • Speak up: if you see behaviour that breaks this Code or makes anyone unsafe, report it as described in Section 9.

4. Prohibited behaviour

The following behaviour is not tolerated in any programme space:

  • Bullying, harassment, intimidation or threats of any kind, including in group chats and private messages between participants;
  • Discrimination or hateful conduct on grounds of ethnicity, religion, gender, disability, background or any other characteristic;
  • Sexualised language, advances or content directed at anyone in the community, and any sexual conduct or communication involving a participant under 18, which will also be reported to the authorities;
  • Sharing violent, obscene, extremist or otherwise harmful content;
  • Fraud, impersonation, or misrepresenting your identity, age or work;
  • Plagiarism: presenting the work of another person, or unacknowledged AI output, as your own;
  • Recording sessions or other participants without authorisation;
  • Soliciting money from, or offering money to, other participants, or promoting schemes, gambling or unrelated commercial ventures in programme spaces;
  • Attempting to gain unauthorised access to programme platforms, accounts or data, or any conduct prohibited by the Cybercrimes (Prohibition, Prevention, etc.) Act 2015 as amended;
  • Retaliating against anyone who raises a concern in good faith.

5. Responsible use of AI tools

Learning to use AI honestly and critically is the heart of this programme, so we hold participants to a clear standard:

  • Disclose it: when AI tools contributed to your assignment or capstone work, say so and describe how. Disclosed use is learning; undisclosed use is plagiarism;
  • Understand it: you must be able to explain and defend everything you submit. The programme exists to close the gap between producing output and understanding it;
  • Verify it: AI outputs can be wrong or biased. Check claims before you rely on or repeat them;
  • Respect people: never use AI tools to create content that deceives, harasses, sexualises or impersonates any real person, and never generate or share fabricated images or recordings of participants, staff or anyone else;
  • Respect rights: do not feed other people's personal data, or confidential or copyrighted material you have no right to use, into AI tools;
  • Follow the tools' own rules: third party AI platforms have their own terms and age requirements, and you must comply with them. Where a tool's minimum age is above yours, the programme will provide a supervised or alternative arrangement;
  • Ask when unsure: if you are not certain whether a use of AI is acceptable, ask a facilitator before submitting.

6. Conduct in online sessions and messaging groups

  • Join sessions with your registered name so facilitators know who is present;
  • Keep chat contributions relevant and respectful; the chat is part of the classroom;
  • Official WhatsApp and messaging groups are for programme announcements, questions and submissions only; do not add outsiders to the groups, forward group content externally, or send broadcast or promotional messages;
  • Do not contact facilitators or mentors on their personal numbers or social media; use official channels. This rule protects both you and them;
  • Cameras and microphones are used as directed by the facilitator, and screenshots of other participants are not permitted without consent;
  • Keep your login details private and tell the programme team at once if you believe your access has been compromised.

7. In person events

At physical workshops and events, follow the directions of programme staff, remain in the designated areas, and observe the venue rules. Participants under 18 must have the required parental consent for attendance and travel arrangements communicated by the programme. Alcohol, illegal substances and weapons are prohibited at all programme events.

8. Your work and our materials

You own the work you create in the programme. The Initiative may use your submissions for teaching, assessment and evaluation, and, only with your separate consent (and a parent or guardian's consent if you are under 18), for showcasing on our website and social media. Programme materials, recordings and templates belong to the Initiative and are for your personal learning; do not republish, sell or teach from them without written permission. Full details are in Section 8 of the Terms of Use.

9. Raising concerns

If anyone, adult or participant, makes you feel unsafe or uncomfortable, or you see behaviour that breaks this Code, report it. You can speak to any facilitator or staff member, contact the Designated Safeguarding Lead named at the start of your cohort, or email programme@venixnextgen.org with the subject line "Concern" (use "Safeguarding" if the matter involves someone's safety). Reports are handled seriously, promptly and as confidentially as possible, and no one will ever be penalised for reporting in good faith. If you are ever in immediate danger, contact emergency services on 112 first.

10. What happens when the Code is broken

Responses are proportionate to the behaviour, and the process is fair. Except where immediate action is needed to protect someone, we will explain the concern to the participant, hear their account, and involve a parent or guardian where the participant is under 18. Possible outcomes include:

  • A private conversation and a reminder of the Code;
  • A formal warning recorded on the programme file;
  • Redoing an assignment where the concern relates to honesty in submitted work;
  • Temporary suspension from sessions or groups;
  • Removal from the cohort and, in serious cases, from the alumni community, with certificates withheld or revoked where completion requirements were not honestly met;
  • Referral to the appropriate authorities where the behaviour may be unlawful or a safeguarding matter.

A participant may ask for a decision to be reviewed by writing to programme@venixnextgen.org within 14 days, and the review will be conducted by a person not involved in the original decision.

11. Acknowledgement

Every participant confirms at enrolment that they have read and accepted this Code. For participants under 18, a parent or guardian confirms acceptance as well. The current version of the Code, with its effective date, is available on the Website, and material changes are communicated to active cohorts.

This Code should be read together with our Terms of Use, Privacy Policy, Cookie Policy and Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy.
